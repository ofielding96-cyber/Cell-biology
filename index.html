1. Plant and Animal Cells · Células animales y vegetales

CELL · Célula

English: A cell is the smallest living part of an organism — the basic building block of all life. Some organisms are made of just one cell (e.g. bacteria); others, like plants and animals, are made of many billions of cells.

Español: Una célula es la parte viva más pequeña de un organismo, el bloque básico de la vida. Algunos organismos tienen una sola célula; otros, como las plantas y los animales, tienen miles de millones.

EUKARYOTIC CELL · Célula eucariota

English: A cell that contains its DNA enclosed inside a nucleus. Animal and plant cells are both eukaryotic cells.

Español: Una célula cuyo ADN está encerrado dentro de un núcleo. Las células animales y vegetales son eucariotas.

KEY EXAM LANGUAGE: nucleus  ·  contains DNA  ·  membrane-bound

Sub-cellular structures (organelles) and their functions

All cells contain smaller structures called organelles that each carry out a specific job. Plant cells have three extra organelles that animal cells do not have.

OrganelleFound inFunction
Nucleus · NúcleoAnimal PlantControls all the activities of the cell — contains the DNA.
Cytoplasm · CitoplasmaAnimal PlantGel-like substance where most chemical reactions take place.
Cell membrane · Membrana celularAnimal PlantControls what substances move into and out of the cell.
Mitochondria · MitocondriasAnimal PlantSite of aerobic respiration — releases energy the cell can use.
Ribosomes · RibosomasAnimal PlantSite of protein synthesis (where proteins are made).
Cell wall · Pared celularPlant onlyMade of cellulose. Strengthens the cell and supports it.
Chloroplasts · CloroplastosPlant onlyContain chlorophyll, which absorbs light energy for photosynthesis.
Permanent vacuole · Vacuola permanentePlant onlyContains cell sap (water + sugars/salts); keeps the cell rigid for support.
KEY EXAM LANGUAGE: "state the function of..." = give a short factual sentence starting with a verb, e.g. "controls...", "releases...", "absorbs..."

Similarities and differences: plant vs animal cells

Similarities

Both have a nucleus, cytoplasm, cell membrane, mitochondria and ribosomes.

Differences

Plant cells also have a cell wall, chloroplasts and a permanent vacuole. Animal cells do not have any of these three.

2. Prokaryotic Cells · Células procariotas

PROKARYOTE · Procariota

English: A prokaryotic cell is much smaller than a eukaryotic cell and does not have a nucleus. Its genetic material (DNA) is found free in the cytoplasm. Bacteria are prokaryotic organisms.

Español: Una célula procariota es mucho más pequeña que una eucariota y no tiene núcleo. Su ADN se encuentra libre en el citoplasma. Las bacterias son organismos procariotas.

KEY EXAM LANGUAGE: no nucleus  ·  genetic material in the cytoplasm  ·  much smaller

3. Magnification and Units · Aumento y unidades

MAGNIFICATION · Aumento

English: Magnification means making an image look bigger than the real (actual) object. Cells are too small to see with the naked eye, so scientists use microscopes to magnify them.

Español: El aumento significa hacer que una imagen se vea más grande que el objeto real. Las células son demasiado pequeñas para verlas a simple vista, así que los científicos usan microscopios.

The magnification triangle

Image size (I), Actual size (A) and Magnification (M) are linked by this equation:

Magnification = Image size ÷ Actual size

Rearranged, this also gives:

Image size = Actual size × Magnification   |   Actual size = Image size ÷ Magnification

KEY EXAM LANGUAGE: "calculate" = show your working with numbers  ·  always give a unit in your final answer

Units and conversions

Size of objectUnit usedExample
Large objectsmetres (m)Field, height of a person
Small objectscentimetres (cm) / millimetres (mm)Small organisms, seeds
Tiny objectsmicrometres (μm) / nanometres (nm)Cells, organelles, microbes
Conversion ladder (learn this!):

1 mm = 1,000 μm   ·   1 μm = 1,000 nm   ·   1 mm = 1,000,000 nm

To go mm → μm, multiply by 1000. To go μm → mm, divide by 1000. Always convert to the same unit before you calculate!

4. Microscopy · Microscopía

Why do we use microscopes? · ¿Por qué usamos microscopios?

English: Cells and their organelles are far too small to see with the naked eye. Microscopes magnify specimens so scientists can study their structure.

Español: Las células y sus orgánulos son demasiado pequeños para verlos a simple vista. Los microscopios aumentan las muestras para poder estudiar su estructura.

Light microscopes · Microscopios ópticos

  • Use a beam of light and glass lenses.
  • Cheap, easy to use, can view living specimens.
  • Magnify up to about ×1500 – ×2000.
  • Specimens must be thin so light passes through; often stained to see structures (most cells are transparent).
  • Cannot resolve very small organelles like ribosomes.

Electron microscopes · Microscopios electrónicos

  • Use a beam of electrons instead of light.
  • Much higher magnification (up to ×2,000,000) and resolution.
  • Can see small organelles (e.g. ribosomes, detail inside mitochondria), sometimes in 3D.
  • Large, very expensive, cannot be used on living specimens.
  • Images often appear black/grey (not in natural colour).
KEY EXAM LANGUAGE — Resolution vs magnification: Magnification = how much bigger the image is. Resolution = how well a microscope can distinguish between two points that are close together (i.e. how much detail/clarity you get).

How microscopy improved our understanding of cells: Because electron microscopes have much greater magnification and resolution than light microscopes, scientists could see organelles (like ribosomes and the internal structure of mitochondria) that are invisible under a light microscope. This let scientists build a far more detailed model of what is inside a cell.

Using a light microscope

Total magnification = eyepiece lens magnification × objective lens magnification

  • Always start with the lowest-power objective lens (e.g. ×4) — it gives the widest field of view, making it easier to find your specimen.
  • Use the coarse focus knob first (moves the stage up/down by a large amount) — only at low power.
  • Switch to a higher-power objective lens, then use the fine focus knob to sharpen the image.
  • Never use the coarse focus knob at high power — it can move the stage too far and smash the slide into the objective lens.

5. Required Practical — Microscopy · Práctica obligatoria – Microscopía

Aim · Objetivo: To prepare a slide of plant cells (onion/epidermal tissue) and observe them using a light microscope, producing a labelled scientific drawing.

Apparatus & specimen preparation

  1. Use a dropping pipette to put one drop of water onto the microscope slide.
  2. Peel off a thin layer of epidermal (skin) tissue from the specimen.
  3. Use forceps to place the thin layer flat onto the slide, on top of the water.
  4. Add 2 drops of iodine solution — this stains the cell structures so they are easier to see. ⚠ Iodine is an irritant — wear eye protection and wash hands if spilled.
  5. Lower a coverslip onto the specimen carefully using a mounted needle (avoids trapping air bubbles).
  6. Use paper towel to soak up any excess liquid.

Viewing the slide

  1. Place the slide on the stage and secure with the stage clips.
  2. Select the lowest-power objective lens first — a smaller field of view at high power makes it hard to locate the cells.
  3. Turn the coarse focus knob to bring the specimen roughly into focus.
  4. Turn the fine focus knob to sharpen the image.
  5. To see more detail, rotate to a higher-power objective lens and refocus using only the fine focus knob.
  6. Make a clear, labelled scientific drawing of what you see.
Safety: handle glass slides and coverslips carefully — they break easily and can cut skin. Iodine is an irritant.
Common mistakes: starting on high power (can't find the specimen); using the coarse focus knob at high power (may crack the slide); forgetting to state the magnification used on your drawing; drawing individual cells with thick, sketchy outlines.

Biological drawing conventions

  • Draw with a sharp pencil, using clear continuous lines (no shading/colouring).
  • Use a ruler to draw straight label lines — they must never cross.
  • Always state the magnification used next to your drawing.
  • Include at least 2–3 cells, drawn to scale relative to each other.

6. DNA, Chromosomes and Genes · ADN, cromosomas y genes

CELL  →  NUCLEUS  →  CHROMOSOME  →  DNA  →  GENE
CÉLULA → NÚCLEO → CROMOSOMA → ADN → GEN

DNA · ADN

English: DNA (deoxyribonucleic acid) is the chemical inside the nucleus that carries an organism's genetic information — the instructions needed to grow, develop and function.

Español: El ADN es el compuesto químico dentro del núcleo que contiene la información genética de un organismo: las instrucciones necesarias para crecer y funcionar.

CHROMOSOME · Cromosoma

English: A structure made of tightly coiled DNA, found in the nucleus. A human body cell contains 46 chromosomes, arranged in 23 pairs. Each chromosome in a pair carries the same type of genes — one of each pair comes from each parent.

Español: Una estructura hecha de ADN muy enrollado, dentro del núcleo. Una célula humana tiene 46 cromosomas en 23 pares. En cada célula, el 50% del ADN se hereda de cada progenitor.

GENE · Gen

English: A gene is a small section of a chromosome (a section of DNA) that codes for one particular protein, and so controls one characteristic of the organism.

Español: Un gen es una pequeña sección de un cromosoma (una parte del ADN) que codifica una proteína concreta y controla una característica.

KEY EXAM LANGUAGE: "a short section of DNA"  ·  "codes for a protein"  ·  "controls a characteristic"

7. Cell Cycle and Mitosis · Ciclo celular y mitosis

MITOSIS · Mitosis

English: A type of cell division that produces two genetically identical daughter cells. Mitosis is used for growth, repair of damaged tissue, and asexual reproduction.

Español: Un tipo de división celular que produce dos células hijas genéticamente idénticas. La mitosis se usa para el crecimiento, la reparación de tejidos y la reproducción asexual.

KEY EXAM LANGUAGE: "genetically identical"  ·  "daughter cells"  ·  "growth and repair"

The three stages of the cell cycle

Before a cell divides, its DNA and organelles must be copied — this ensures both daughter cells end up with a full, identical set.

1. Growth (Interphase) · Interfase

  • DNA is copied — two identical copies of each chromosome are produced.
  • The number of sub-cellular structures (ribosomes, mitochondria) increases.
  • The cell grows in size.

2. Mitosis · Mitosis

  • The chromosomes are separated.
  • One full set of chromosomes is pulled to each end of the cell.
  • The nucleus divides — two new nuclei form.

3. Cytokinesis · Citocinesis

  • The cytoplasm and cell membrane divide.
  • Two separate, genetically identical daughter cells are formed.
Don't mix these up!
TermWhat actually happens
DNA replicationCopying the DNA so there are two identical sets — happens during the growth stage, before mitosis.
MitosisThe nucleus divides — chromosomes are separated into two identical sets.
CytokinesisThe cytoplasm and cell membrane divide, forming two separate daughter cells.

Why is mitosis important? It allows multicellular organisms to grow (more cells = bigger body) and to repair damaged tissue by replacing dead or injured cells with genetically identical new ones.

8. Diffusion · Difusión

DIFFUSION · Difusión

English: The net movement of particles from an area of higher concentration to an area of lower concentration, down a concentration gradient. Diffusion is a passive process — it does not require energy.

Español: El movimiento neto de partículas desde una zona de mayor concentración hacia una zona de menor concentración, a favor del gradiente de concentración. La difusión es un proceso pasivo — no necesita energía.

KEY EXAM LANGUAGE: net movement  ·  higher concentration  ·  lower concentration  ·  concentration gradient

Particles in a gas or liquid move constantly in random directions. This random movement is passive (no energy needed), but overall — on net — more particles end up moving from high to low concentration until the particles are evenly spread (equilibrium).

Factors affecting the rate of diffusion

Concentration gradient

A bigger difference in concentration between two areas means faster diffusion.

Temperature

Higher temperature → particles have more kinetic energy → they move faster → diffusion is faster.

Surface area

A larger surface area gives more space for particles to cross, so more diffusion can happen in the same time.

Exchange surfaces & their adaptations

Surfaces where substances diffuse in/out of organisms (e.g. lungs, small intestine, gills, roots) are adapted to maximise the rate of diffusion:

  • Large surface area — more space for diffusion to happen across.
  • Thin membrane/surface — short diffusion distance.
  • Good blood supply / ventilation — maintains a steep concentration gradient by constantly removing/replenishing substances.

Examples: oxygen and glucose diffuse into cells; carbon dioxide and urea diffuse out as waste.

9. Surface Area : Volume Ratio · Relación superficie : volumen

SURFACE AREA : VOLUME RATIO · Relación SA:V

English: As an organism (or cell) gets bigger, its surface area to volume ratio gets smaller. This matters because the rate of exchange (diffusion) depends on surface area, but the demand for substances depends on volume.

Español: A medida que un organismo (o célula) crece, su relación superficie:volumen se hace más pequeña. Esto importa porque el intercambio (difusión) depende del área, pero la demanda depende del volumen.

Small organisms (e.g. bacteria, single-celled organisms) have a large SA:V ratio — every part is close to the surface, so diffusion alone is fast enough to supply/remove substances. Large organisms (e.g. mammals) have a small SA:V ratio — diffusion alone would be too slow to reach the centre of the body, so they need specialised exchange surfaces (lungs, gills, intestine villi) and transport systems (blood/circulatory system).

KEY EXAM LANGUAGE — sentence scaffold:
"As the size of the organism increases, the surface area to volume ratio ______. This means diffusion becomes ______, so larger organisms need ______."

"A medida que aumenta el tamaño del organismo, la relación superficie:volumen ______. Esto significa que la difusión se vuelve ______, por lo que los organismos grandes necesitan ______."

10. Osmosis · Ósmosis

OSMOSIS · Ósmosis

English: The net movement of water molecules from a region of higher water concentration to a region of lower water concentration, through a partially permeable membrane.

Español: El movimiento neto de moléculas de agua desde una zona de mayor concentración de agua hacia una zona de menor concentración de agua, a través de una membrana parcialmente permeable.

KEY EXAM LANGUAGE: movement of WATER  ·  partially permeable membrane  ·  high to low water concentration
🚩 Common misconception: Osmosis is not the same as diffusion! Diffusion can move any small particle (e.g. oxygen, CO₂, glucose) and doesn't need a special membrane. Osmosis is specifically about water moving through a partially permeable membrane.

Dilute vs concentrated solutions

Dilute solution · Solución diluida

High water concentration, low solute concentration.

Concentrated solution · Solución concentrada

Low water concentration, high solute concentration.

Water always moves from dilute → concentrated (i.e. from high to low water concentration). An isotonic solution has the same water concentration as the cell, so there is no net movement of water.

Effects of osmosis on cells

SolutionAnimal cellPlant cell
Dilute / pure waterWater enters → cell swells → may burst / lyse (no cell wall to stop it)Water enters → cell swells → becomes turgid (cell wall stops it bursting)
IsotonicNo net movement — no changeNo net movement — no change
ConcentratedWater leaves → cell shrinks / crenatedWater leaves → membrane pulls away from wall → plasmolysed / flaccid

Why don't plant cells burst? The cellulose cell wall is strong and elastic — it pushes back against the swelling vacuole and membrane, so the cell becomes rigid (turgid) instead of bursting.

11. Required Practical — Osmosis · Práctica obligatoria – Ósmosis

Aim: To investigate the effect of different sugar solution concentrations on the mass of plant tissue (e.g. potato or aubergine cylinders).
Independent variable

Concentration of the sugar solution (e.g. 0, 0.25, 0.5, 0.75, 1.0 mol/dm³).

Dependent variable

Change in mass of the plant tissue.

Control variables

Type of plant tissue, size/volume of cylinders, temperature, time left in solution.

Method

  1. Cut cylinders of plant tissue to the same diameter (cork borer) and trim to the same length — remove the skin, as it is impermeable.
  2. Blot each cylinder dry and record its initial mass.
  3. Place one cylinder into each of five sugar solutions of different concentration (10 cm³ each).
  4. Leave for a set time (e.g. 20 minutes).
  5. Remove each cylinder, blot dry again, and record the final mass.
  6. Calculate the change in mass and the percentage change in mass for each cylinder.
Safety: take care with a sharp knife/cork borer when cutting the plant tissue.

Percentage change in mass

change in mass = final mass − initial mass

% change in mass = (change in mass ÷ initial mass) × 100

12. Active Transport · Transporte activo

ACTIVE TRANSPORT · Transporte activo

English: The movement of particles from a region of lower concentration to a region of higher concentration — i.e. against the concentration gradient. This requires energy, which comes from respiration in the mitochondria.

Español: El movimiento de partículas desde una zona de menor concentración hacia una zona de mayor concentración, es decir, en contra del gradiente de concentración. Necesita energía, que proviene de la respiración en las mitocondrias.

KEY EXAM LANGUAGE: low to high concentration  ·  against the concentration gradient  ·  requires energy from respiration

Root hair cells · Células pilíferas

Plants need mineral ions (e.g. nitrate) for healthy growth, but soil often has a lower concentration of these ions than inside the root cell. Root hair cells actively transport mineral ions against the concentration gradient, using energy from respiration. They contain many mitochondria to supply this energy.

Intestinal epithelial cells · Células epiteliales intestinales

Once most glucose has diffused out of the gut, its concentration can become lower in the gut than in the blood/cells. Active transport allows cells lining the small intestine to continue absorbing glucose against its concentration gradient.
